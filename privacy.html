Privacy policy

Effective September 1, 2026 · updated October 21, 2026

bridge.games is a free site for playing contract bridge, run independently. This page says what we collect, what we do with it, and — mostly — what we don’t do. Questions: hello@bridge.games.

What we collect

  • Your account: an email address, a username, and a password (stored hashed — we cannot read it).
  • Your profile, if you fill it in: real name, country, bio, photo, bidding style, and convention cards. Profiles and convention cards are visible to other players by design — that’s how opponents read your system, as at any club.
  • Your play: every board you play is recorded — calls, cards, results — so hands can be replayed, scored against the field, and reviewed. A table’s log also records seating changes, undos, and claims.
  • Your connections: friends, follows, and blocks you set up, and the clubs you belong to.
  • Your settings: preferences such as table colour, card style, your time zone (taken from your browser until you change it), who may send you messages, and which emails you want — saved to your account so every device and every email gets them right.
  • Messages to Support: when you write to Support, your message is kept, and a copy goes to our inbox, so we can answer it and look back at it — like Send feedback.
  • Technical basics: ordinary server logs (such as IP addresses, and the size of your browser window when you open a table); when table video misbehaves, connection diagnostics that help us fix it; and short reports from your page of what it is showing — the table and board, whose turn it is, what is open on the screen and the window’s size, never a card. A page sends one when you arrive at a tournament table, when a table has waited a minute or more on your turn, just before a stuck page reloads itself, and when our support team asks while helping with a problem. They go into the same logs and nowhere else.
  • Visit counts: this website counts its visitors with GoatCounter, a privacy-respecting counter. Per visit it keeps the page, the site that sent you, your browser and system family, screen width, and the country your connection appears to come from. If you click one of our sign-up buttons, it counts which one. Your IP address is used only to work out that country and to tell visits apart for a few hours; it is not stored, no cookie is set, nothing is kept in your browser, and nothing is tied to you or to an account (GoatCounter’s own policy).

What we never do

  • No advertising, and no advertising trackers.
  • No analytics that follow you around the web — our visit counter sees only this site.
  • No selling, renting, or sharing your data with anyone.
  • No payment details — the site is free, so we never ask.

Emails we send

We email you about your account (signing up, resetting a password). Unless you turn them off under Email notifications in your settings, we also email you when you’re invited to an event or asked to be someone’s partner, and ten minutes before an event you’re entered in starts if you aren’t already on the site — one reminder per event.

Club invitations. A club’s owner or managers can invite people by pasting a list of email addresses, including people who aren’t on bridge.games yet. Each address gets one email from bridge.games — “Ann invited you to join Monday Night Bridge on bridge.games” — with a link that works once. We use the address only for that invitation. Only the club’s managers, who typed it, see it. Every invitation ends with an Unsubscribe link, and your mail app’s own Unsubscribe button works too. The link asks first and changes nothing until you press the button; once an address is unsubscribed, no club on bridge.games can send it another invitation. We keep an unsubscribed address only so we can keep that promise. A club can email at most 200 new addresses a day. Got one and want your address removed? Write to hello@bridge.games.

Your address on what you send. An invitation you send — to a club, or to an event you direct — and the reminders for an event you host carry your email address as the reply-to, so an answer reaches you. That is the only way another person sees your email address here, and only because you sent the invitation or run the event.

Video and voice

Voice and video are off at a table until whoever creates it, its captain, or an event’s director switches them on, and your own camera and microphone stay off until you turn them on. Table video and voice travel directly between the players at your table, encrypted, peer to peer. When a network requires it, the encrypted stream may pass through our relay server — still unreadable to us. Nothing is ever recorded or stored, and we couldn’t watch if we wanted to.

Chat

Chat isn’t kept. What you write at a table, in the lounge, in an event’s chat or in a direct message lives only in our servers’ memory, goes to the people you addressed, and is gone when the site next updates; it is never saved to our database. A message to Support is the exception, kept as described above. At a tournament table, the event’s directors can read what is written while a hand is being played, messages to the opponents included — as a director at a real table would hear it.

Cookies and storage

This website sets no cookies at all. The app keeps a few things in your browser: your sign-in token, which is how the server knows it is you, and small notes about how you like the screen — table colour, card style, your Card size (kept on that device only, so each device can have its own; it is never sent to us), whether the lounge bar is open, whether the drawer is pinned — so the page paints right before it has heard from us. Some of those preferences are also saved to your account.

What other players see

Your username, profile, presence at tables, and results are part of playing bridge with other people — anyone at your table or in the lobby can see them, the Stats page shows who is online right now and whether they are playing, watching or just around, and boards are scored against everyone who played the same cards. Your unplayed cards go where the rules of the game and the table’s settings send them, and nowhere else: a player sees their own hand and dummy’s; dummy sees declarer’s; whoever answers a claim sees all four; spectators and directors see what the table allows; everyone sees the deal once the board is over. Our bots see a single seat, except the Double Dummy bot, which is marked as such and whose boards count in no one’s comparisons.

In a club, its members see who else belongs; a private club’s page, members, tables and events are for its members alone. A club’s owner and managers can download its member list — usernames, the real names members put in their profiles, roles and the dates they joined — never email addresses. And the people who run bridge.games can look in on any table or event, private ones included, to answer a call for help or put a problem right.

Where your data lives, and your rights

Data is stored on our servers in the United States. Email hello@bridge.games to ask for a copy of your data or to delete your account. One honest caveat: results already scored into a board’s field stay in the scoring history (removing them would change other players’ scores), but we’ll disconnect them from your identity.

Age

bridge.games is not directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their information.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version appears here with a new date. We won’t quietly start doing any of the things listed under “what we never do.”
