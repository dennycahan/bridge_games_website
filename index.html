1NT Balanced · cards your size

Cards as big as your screen allows.

Bridge is easier to enjoy when you can read it at a glance. Under Settings, Card size has Normal, Large and Extra large. Large spreads the hands into the corners and moves your buttons onto your name bar; Extra large also folds the page header away while you play. The size is kept on each device, so your iPad and your laptop can each have their own.

  • Change it where you sit. Card size is in Settings, in the table’s ☰ menu and in your profile menu, and the table redraws as you choose.
  • Tiles, for bigger still. Under Hands, the Tiles style draws each card as just its rank and suit, side by side, which lets them be bigger again.
  • Full screen in one tap. At the top of any page, or in the menu at a table, it gives the whole screen to the cards; tap it again to come back. (An iPhone’s browser has no full screen.)
  • Like an app on your iPad. Add bridge.games to your home screen and it opens without the browser’s bars — on an iPhone too. Phones keep their own layout, built for your thumbs.
2♦ Chat, voice & video

Your regular table, wherever everyone lives.

Talk the way you would across a real table. Chat with everyone, with your partner between hands, or with either opponent. Turn on voice and video, and the table looks back: four faces, one in each corner, seated where they’d really be sitting. Say “well played” out loud. Groan at a bad split together.

  • Pick who hears you. Table, Partner (between hands), Opponents, your LHO or RHO by name, or the kibitzers — and the director, at an event. Stuck? Choose Support and the people who run the site answer you right there.
  • Write to a friend anywhere. A direct message reaches any member who’s online, in the chat on whatever page they’re on. Friends show in green, and a soft chime tells you when a message is for you.
  • Voice and video when your table wants them. They’re off until whoever creates the table ticks Voice and video, or the captain turns them on. Once they’re on, you see and hear whoever turns on a camera or microphone from the moment you sit, and yours stay off until you choose.
  • Video never covers the cards. Tiles keep to the corners — or, at the larger card sizes, to the side panel — and the felt stays clear.
  • Player to player wherever the network allows. Your video and voice go straight to your tablemates, encrypted. When a firewall won’t permit a direct path, the encrypted stream takes our relay instead — still unreadable to us.

3♥ Tournaments & hosting

Run the game you wish existed nearby.

The lobby lists every event with entries open — step into one solo, or enter with a partner by name. And when you’d rather run the game than wait for one, hosting is a button, not a committee: name your event, choose matchpoints or IMPs, set the rounds and the clock, and the site seats every pair, moves them each round, and keeps live standings. It even tells you when it will end at the latest. Prefer head-to-head? Set up a team match: two tables, one scorecard, IMPs.

  • Enter alone, play with a partner. Solo entrants are paired at start time, a bot partners the odd one out, and bot pairs make up the field to at least two tables — nobody registers and sits out.
  • Invite your crowd. Invite players by name on the event page, or a whole saved list in one press. Each invitation is emailed, and everyone entered gets a reminder ten minutes before the start if they aren’t on the site. A private event shows in the lobby only for its own people.
  • Round after round, no waiting. Players walk from one round’s table straight to the next. A round moves on ten seconds after its last table finishes, or at the clock, and the last board takes everyone to the results.
  • Real duplicate scoring. Matchpoints as a percentage of the field, or cross-IMPs on the standard scale.
4♥ The captain’s table

Every table has a captain. Make it yours.

Open a table and you hold the anchor. Name it if you like; choose who may join and watch, whether undos are allowed, and whether voice and video are on; press a chair to seat yourself or invite a friend into it. Give it a start time and it waits under Booked, then opens itself. And you can change your mind mid-session, without starting over.

  • Settings in plain words. Who can join, what happens at the table, and how it’s scored — each with a ? that says what it does, and all of it changeable later from Table settings.
  • Who sits is your call. Leave “Players may seat themselves” on and anyone can take an empty chair from the lobby. Turn it off and your table shows a padlock; “Alice wants to sit North” pops up wherever you are, with nine seconds to seat them or not.
  • The captain’s menu. Skip a board, reset the score, seat bots — BenBot, FastBen (quicker, and slightly weaker) or Double Dummy — remove a kibitzer, or review the log: every call, card and seat change, timestamped.
  • The essentials stay at hand. Claim and Undo sit under the table, or right on your name bar at the larger card sizes (undos still ask your opponents’ consent).
4♠ Scoring & fair play

Serious about the game part.

Every hand you play is kept: the full deal, the auction, the play card by card, and how you did against everyone who held your cards. And the deal itself is guarded the way a club director would want.

  • Your browser only ever receives what your seat is entitled to. What reaches a screen depends on the role, the state of the hand and the table’s settings: a player sees their own hand, and dummy once the lead is faced; dummy sees declarer’s; whoever answers a claim sees all four; spectators and directors see what the table allows; everyone sees the whole deal once the board is over. The server holds the rest.
  • Most bots see exactly what you see. They read the same single-seat view a person gets. The one exception is marked: the Double Dummy bot plays with all four hands showing, and any board it sits at is flagged and kept out of everyone’s comparisons.
  • Fresh boards, always. You’re never dealt a board you’ve already played — or even watched.
  • A tournament is as quiet as its director wants. Voice and video are switches on the event, off until the director turns them on: on for a club night among friends, off where partners could trade more than bids. Private messages into a table that’s still playing are off too, unless the director allows them.
4NT Asking · replay any hand

Play any hand again, your way.

Any hand you’ve played or watched can be played again, against the bots, at a private table of your own. Jump to the auction or the lead, step a trick or a card either way, let the record play itself — then take over from any card and try the line you wish you’d found. Nothing about a replay counts.

  • Full controls, on the felt. Auction, lead, back a trick or a card, forward, auto play, show or hide the hands, and the way out — every control sits in the bar above the table, and you play whichever hand is on turn.
  • From the table you’re sitting at. Open any board in the scorecard and press Replay mode. Your seat is held while you experiment, and you come back to it when you’re done.
  • Bookmark it for later. The star on any replay keeps the hand, with a note, under Bookmarked Boards. Open it from there whenever you like, and flip between your bookmarks without leaving the table.
  • All four hands on show. A replay opens with every hand face up, so you can see why a line works before you try it — or hide them and test yourself.
5♣ Clubs · bring everyone

Your own club, for your own members.

A club on bridge.games has its own page with the same tables, booked tables and tournaments as the lobby — for its members. Make it public, open to anyone or with your approval, or private: by invitation, with its page, members and games for members only.

  • Bring everyone at once. Paste your members’ email addresses. Anyone not yet on bridge.games gets a link that signs them up and puts them straight in the club.
  • Share the work. Any member can open a table in the club. You and your managers host its tournaments and approve who joins.

7NT Bidding you in · how to start

Three calls and you’re playing.

  1. Create your free account

    A username, an email, a password — you’re in the lobby in under a minute. Nothing to install, nothing to pay.

  2. Take a seat

    Press Sit on any open chair in the lobby, or Play MPs to be dealt in at once with three bots. Or create a table and invite friends to its chairs — tick Voice and video if you’d like to see each other.

  3. Play, and see how you did

    Every board scored against the field, every hand kept for replay. When you’re ready, host a tournament — or bring your whole club.

Alert Questions, explained

Fair questions, straight answers.

Is it really free?
Yes. No subscription, no deal limits, nothing extra for bots, tournaments, clubs, or video. A free account is all we ask for.
Is there anything to download?
No. bridge.games runs in your web browser on a computer, tablet, or phone — nothing to install, nothing to update. On an iPad or iPhone you can add it to your home screen, and it opens like an app. Video and voice need a computer or tablet; phones play cards-only.
Can I play bridge against the computer?
Yes. Press Play MPs or Play IMPs in the lobby and you’re dealt in at once, with a bot in each of the other three seats — your partner and both opponents — any hour of the day. Or mix and match: one bot to make a fourth, two to make up a table with a friend.
Do I need an account?
Yes, a free one: a username, an email, and a password, and you’re in the lobby in under a minute. Your account is what keeps every hand you play and scores it against the field.
Can I play with my friends?
Yes. Create a table, press a chair to invite a friend into it, and tick Voice and video if you’d like to see each other. Or host a tournament for your whole crowd.
Can I talk to my partner?
Between hands, yes: choose Partner in the chat. While a hand is being played, partner chat is closed, as it is across a real table — but you can talk to the whole table or the opponents at any time. With voice on, everyone at the table hears what’s said.
Does it work on a tablet?
Yes, and it’s a fine way to play. Choose Large or Extra large under Card size and the cards fill the screen; tap Full screen, or add bridge.games to your home screen, and the browser’s bars go too. Each device keeps its own card size.
Can my club play here?
Yes. Start a club under Clubs, next to Lobby, and it gets its own page with its own tables, booked tables and tournaments. Make it public or private, and invite your members by name or by pasting their email addresses. More for clubs.
Can I play a hand again?
Yes. Any hand you’ve played or watched can be replayed at a private table against the bots, from any card, with all four hands showing. Star it to keep it under Bookmarked Boards for later. Nothing played in a replay counts.
Is it duplicate bridge?
Yes. Every board, at a casual table or in a tournament, is scored against everyone on the site who played the same cards — matchpoints or IMPs — and kept for replay.
