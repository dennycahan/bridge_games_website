2♦ Video & voice at the table

See your friends, not just their cards.

Turn on your camera and the table looks back: four faces, one in each corner, seated where they’d really be sitting. Say “well played” out loud. Groan at a bad split together. It’s your regular table — wherever everyone happens to live.

  • One click to join. You see and hear the table from the moment you sit down; your own camera stays off until you choose.
  • Video never covers the cards. Tiles keep to the corners — the felt stays clear, whoever joins.
  • Player to player, not through us. Your video and voice go directly to your tablemates, never through our servers.
  • Computers and tablets today. Phones play cards-only for now, and tournament tables are deliberately silent — fair play first.

3♥ Tournaments & hosting

Run the game you wish existed nearby.

Hosting a tournament is a button, not a committee. Name your event, choose matchpoints or cross-IMPs, set the rounds and the clock — the site seats every pair, moves them each round, and keeps live standings.

  • Nobody sits out. Solo entrants are paired at start time, and a bot partners the odd one out — every round plays.
  • Real duplicate scoring. Matchpoints as a percentage of the field, or cross-IMPs on the standard scale.
  • The event runs itself. A round clock at every table, automatic seating, and final standings the moment the last board scores.
4♥ The captain’s table

Every table has a captain. Make it yours.

Open a table and you hold the anchor: you seat people and bots, approve who may sit, decide what kibitzers can see and say, allow or bar undos, and can flip the table private or rated — all mid-session, without starting over.

  • Table settings, live. Scoring, privacy, rated play, kibitzer rules, undos — changed from your chair whenever the table agrees it’s time.
  • The captain’s menu. Skip a board, fill empty seats with bots, remove a kibitzer, or review the log — every call, card, and seat change, timestamped.
  • The essentials stay at hand. Claim and undo sit in the command bar (undos still ask your opponents’ consent).
4♠ Scoring & fair play

Serious about the game part.

Every hand you play is kept: the full deal, the auction, the play card by card, and how you did against everyone who held your cards. And the deal itself is guarded the way a club director would want.

  • Your browser only ever receives your own hand. The server holds the deal; there is nothing on your screen to peek at.
  • Bots see exactly what you see. They read the same single-seat view a person gets — cheating isn’t switched off, it’s impossible by design.
  • Fresh boards, always. You’re never dealt a board you’ve already played — or even watched.
  • Tournament tables are silent on purpose. No video or voice where partners could trade more than bids.
